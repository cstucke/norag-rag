## PC Gamer

* Published: May 28th 2013
* PC Gamer talking with **Ben Falcone** from Endnight Games
* original link to the interview
* this interview is not question-answer based as it was presumably conversational

> *Our focus is much more on a survival horror experience, letting players experience being in the world of 'The Hills Have Eyes' or an 80's Italian cannibal film.*
> *We want the players to feel empathy for them [enemies]. We've taken some inspiration here from the novel 'I Am Legend'. There is some question as to who is actually the enemy: them, or you. You're the one who is invading their forest.*
> *Food and water are both really important, there are lots of lakes you can drink from, but if you're hungry you have to either hunt/trap small animals, or plant your own garden.*
> *We're trying to build in a lot of interactivity in everything, we have no traditional story or cutscenes so our focus needs to be on making the world as dynamic and emergent as possible. We want it to be engaging just to spend time in the world.*
> *During cold nights you'll need to either make a fire or stay inside to keep warm. You can sleep at this point, however you should hope you've set up some traps or defenses outside first.*
> *One of our big features is that every tree in the game world can be chopped down, drastically changing the forest permanently. (although chopping down every tree would probably take months to do).*
> *We have a set number of enemies you are battling, not endlessly spawning creatures coming from thin air.*
> *You're hiding huddled in a corner and watching them search for you.*
> *Again, like I Am Legend it's possible to spend the days thinning out the enemies numbers, and players can if they wish and are able to wipe them all out.*
> *Enemies are pretty smart ... we found the game's scariest moments come when you're hiding huddled in a corner and watching them search for you.*
> *Some players will want to just spend time building up a base and defending against enemies, others will want to go explore and find new things about the gameworld; We have a bunch of stuff for players to uncover.*
> *It's [Oculus Rift] a perfect fit; Exploring the forest during the day becomes at times a magical experience, and at night or deep in a random cave a terrifying nightmare.*
> *Since it's so new there are a bunch of challenges to overcome. Scale was the most surprising thing. The first time I walked out of the plane into the forest with the rift on, everything looked wrong.*
> *Small logs looked massive, the enemy characters looked like miniatures. Getting scale correct and consistent is one of the biggest things in making the game world feel real.*
> *We're hoping we can get this working well since it massively enhances the feeling of being connected to the gameworld.*

## This Is My Joystick!

* Published: Jun. 21st 2013
* This Is My Joystick! talking with **Ben Falcone** from Endnight Games
* original link to the interview

**1. Tell us a little bit about the background of the company and why you chose to build *The Forest* on PC.**

> *Our first game was for iPad called End Night. It gave me a chance to explore ideas I had about an open world horror game with procedural elements; however, I was never able to overcome the limitations of touch controls. For The Forest the initial vision was a large procedural forest, and I knew this wouldn’t be possible to the level I was interested in on a tablet or portable device.*
> *We’re also really excited about the Oculus Rift and so our game is designed to use that to create the scariest game experience ever made.*

**2. What kind of challenges have you come across when developing a game for the Steam Community? Are there any less restrictions than iOS, XBLA or PSN?**

> *No challenges so far; the Greenlight community has been amazing and really embraced the type of game we’re making.*

**3. What was your inspiration for The Forest?**

> *I take a lot of inspiration from 70’s and early eighties exploitation films. I Spit On Your Grave, The Hills Have Eyes and Italian cannibal films. The goal was to take a serious and scary horror world and throw players in there. The type of vision we don’t see in today’s games.*

**4. Are you fans of horror movies and games? Any stand-out favourites?**

> *I’m a big fan of horror films; the original versions of Dawn of the Dead, Halloween and Texas Chainsaw Massacre, along with Italian horror movies from the same era, Deep Red, New York Ripper and The Beyond.*
> *My favourite game is still System Shock 2.  I think of all the horror games I’ve played it gets the balance perfectly between scary and calm moments.*

**5. Upon seeing the trailer, I immediately thought Lost + The Descent + Minecraft + first-person survival horror. Is this a fair assumption?**

> *Yep. Completely. I feel these different ideas have never been brought together in a way like this before, and are the perfect premise and set up for a survival game.*

**6. How do you feel about The Forest being compared to Minecraft?**

> *I think it’s great. I’m a massive fan of Minecraft.  Our game is different in a lot of ways as we focus much more on survival horror.  Dropping players into a terrifying world and letting them figure out how to stay alive.*

**7. Could this be considered as Minecraft for the over eighteens?**

> *I think horror can appeal to a pretty wide range of players.*

**8. How do you want the Forest to be perceived by gamers?**

> *I hope players really do find it the scariest game experience they’ve ever had. And with the Oculus Rift I want players to be so scared they can’t even put on the headset.*

**9. How did you come to start developing The Forest with the Rift headset support?**

> *I’ve been really excited for the Rift since it was first announced. Early test versions of The Forest were played using the Sony HMZ-T1 which gives a really immersive 3D view, and even with the limited FOV and lack of head tracking was a pretty amazing experience.*

**10. What challenges are you having to overcome with designing the game with the Rift headset in mind?**

> *Getting the controls right is the biggest right now. Also making sure the scale of the world feels correct; this is something that you don’t really notice until you stand inside the world looking directly at objects.*

## Only Single Player

* Published: Aug. 1st 2013
* article link on WebArchive

**1. For those of us who don't know exactly what The Forest is, can you please give us a quick synopsis of what the game is?**

> *The Forest is an open world horror game, taking inspiration from the late 70’s italian cannibal films. The game throws you into a violent dangerous world, and gives you complete freedom in how you try to survive.*

**2. Does the game have a main storyline to it, or is the goal of the game just to survive as long as you can?**

> *We don’t have a traditional game story. Our focus is on world building, and creating a believable situation.*

**3. Can you explain how the building elements work and how players can use the environment?**

> *Every tree in the game world can be chopped down, and from the wood players can create different structures.*

**4. How do the survival elements work and how in-depth are they? Will players have to, say, boil the water they find to make it drinkable etc.?**

> *You can collect water and boil it, or risk drinking it straight from a water source. Food works in a similar way, if you manage to catch a rabbit the next step is to build a fire and cook the meat. Leave the dead rabbit sitting in the sun too long and it will start to rot, attract flies and make you ill if you try and eat it.*

**5. How do both the elements of the game, survival and survival-horror mix into the game. Are they day and night times equally timed, or do you give players a bit more time during the day then at night?**

> *The enemies mostly come out at night, but if you go down into the caves and start butchering them while they sleep, they’re going to get mad, and they’re going to come for you regardless of the time of day.*
> *It’s also possible to sleep during the nights, so if you’ve set up proper defenses or you’re sleeping on a houseboat you’ve built, you can avoid most nights all together.*

**6. How does crafting work. We read how players can take a stick and sharpen it on a rock to keep the player immersed, rather than going into a menu and selecting an upgrade, but is it just like a quick time event or what?**

> *We don’t have any quicktime events. The in world crafting is instead really simple, you can bring a stick to a fire, and press a key to combine the two, this creates a lit stick which functions as a lightsource. Creating a spear, and other world items is similar.*

**7. How scary would you say the game is? Seems like you have a bit of competition this year with games like Outlast and Amnesia: A Machine for Pigs.**

> *We’re really focused on creating the scariest game possible. We hope having the contrast between calm tranquil moments, such as cooking by a fire, or watching the sunset over the ocean, will make the scary moments even more terrifying.*

**8. Describe for us the Mutant like creatures that we saw in the reveal trailer for the game.**

> *We’re keeping most details about them a secret for players to uncover.*

**9. How do players defend themselves from the creatures?**

> *You can fight them directly with spears, axes and other weapons you make. Or you can spend the day setting up traps and other defenses.*
> *With our crafting system it’s possible to make more advanced weapons. Combine a can of hairspray you find in some plane luggage with your lighter and you have a mini-flamethrower.*

**10. How do you plan to have an immersive gallery of enemies that seem to have real lives while also keeping them scary?**

> *I think giving the enemies emotions and making them realistic seeming actually makes them much scarier.*

**11. What films and books have given you inspiration for The Forest? Hatchet was the first novel that came to mind when seeing the plane crash and the survivalist elements within the first few moments of the trailer.**

> *Italian horror films are a big influence on the game, the core idea of the game is taking a situation, like one of those late 70’s italian cannibal films and putting a player in there. Letting them see how they can survive.*
> *The novel ‘I Am Legend’ is also a big influence on the game, along with other horror films from the 70’s and early 80’s such as texas chainsaw massacre, hills have eyes, the beyond and many others.*

**12. Once you release the game on PC, do you think there's a possibility we could see the game come to the PS4 or Xbox One, or both? It seems like this game would fit perfectly with Sony's Move and Microsoft's Kinect.**

> *Our focus is on the PC version currently, however we’d definitely be interested in console versions down the line.*

**13. Do you hope to turn The Forest into a franchise? Or maybe add DLC later on to try using new environments to survive in?**

> *We’re planning on adding free updates to the game once it’s released. We currently have an experimental randomly generated game mode where players can generate a new world each time they play. This is something i could see us developing further once the game is released.*

**14. When can we expect to play The Forest?**

> *We don’t have a firm release date, but we’re aiming for late 2013.*

**15. As a site that covers primarily the single player experience, as a developer, how do you think the single player experience will change with the next-generation?**

> *I’m most excited about the Oculus rift, and the new types of games that it makes possible. More immersive and realistic games with less of a focus on fast paced action and shuffling players from one exhausting cinematic sequence to another.*
> *For the Forest, we’re heavily focused on making the best Virtual Reality game we can. Things like keeping all the crafting in the game world instead of via menu’s are a direct result of our focus on the oculus rift.*
> *There’s a scene in most cannibal and zombie films where someone is torn open and eaten alive with intestines spilled and pulled out. Picture that scene happening to you. With the rift on you can look down at your characters body as it’s pulled and torn apart. It’s going to be horrifying.*

**16. Any other details about The Forest you would like to share with us? Maybe a new gameplay clip or screenshots?**

> *We’ve made a lot of improvements to the visuals since the first trailer release, and planning a big update showing off the new lighting and improved art soon.*

**17. Thank you for taking the time to answer our questions. We cannot wait to get our hands on The Forest and try it out. If you have any final comments for our readers feel free to say whatever is on your mind!**

> *Thanks for the time!*

## Eurogamer

* Published: Jan. 13th 2014
* Eurogamer talking with **Ben Falcone** and **Michael Mellor** from Endnight Games
* original link to the interview
* this interview is not question-answer based as it was conversational

> *I've been frustrated with games for the past five or six years constantly telling you what to do.*
> *It sometimes feels like work playing a game where you have a backlog of missions you have to do, constantly being bothered by NPCs. We wanted to create a world where we could drop the player into and give them free range to do whatever they want, similar to something like Minecraft where a lot of the fun comes from decisions that you make. We wanted to capture that but do it in a world that looked realistic.*
> *There is a way to end it, but I think a lot of players will play without ending the game or even realising that there is an ending.*
> *Our goal was a never-ending type game - with an ending [laughs].*
> *You might see a landmark off to the distance that you can't necessarily get to yet. You'll have to build up a certain number of skills and equipment to actually get to some parts of the world.*
> *We're hoping we can provide some curiosity, some passive goals, to give players reasons to go out and figure out more about the world.*
> *Players actually can uncover where these mutants came from and who they are and what the forest actually is. But we don't push players in any way to actually do any of that.*
> *If you're in a real forest with crazy cannibals, some of them would try to protect each other. That's something games don't even attempt. They treat all enemies as mindless.*
> *You've invaded their forest, you're murdering them, you're chopping down all their trees. Maybe they're just trying to survive in this forest. Maybe you're the bad guy. So we try to make it emotional when you kill one of them and have them care. They're not all bad, even if they are trying to eat you.*
> *A big problem with random generation is keeping areas interesting; So we have a really buggy randomly generated mode and we're not sure if it's going to make it into the final game. If we can get it working it will be as an additional mode, but the meat of the game will be this more authored, large island.*
> *Our focus is on the single-player game, but we've also been experimenting with co-op as well, which we did think would be fun.*
> *The big issue is keeping the horror element... we tried to maintain that balance where it's still scary if you're playing in co-op mode, which is a tricky thing to get right.*

## G33king Out

* Published: Jan. 22th 2014
* G33king Out talking with **Ben Falcone** from Endnight Games
* archived link to the interview

**1. First, the obvious: What were the main inspirations for The Forest?**

> *The main inspiration came from the cannibal horror films of the 70’s and 80’s. I was never really a fan of those films but the imagery stuck with me and I thought it would make a great game world setting.*

**2. How has the game matured since its beginning stages? For example, was the idea to have it set in a forest with cannibals always the main concept the team was aiming to build, or did it start as anything different?**

> *The idea was always a forest, with the ability to chop down every tree and drastically change the game world. Originally we experimented with different enemies, and at one point we had more supernatural ghost based enemies instead of the cannibal mutants, but it never felt right for the violent horror game we we’re trying to create.*

**3. You've said that the team working on the game is about 3 people large, including yourself. Who are the others, and what role does each member have?**

> *I’m the creative director/programmer and lead designer. We also have Anna, who focuses mostly on 2d/3d art, and Mike our animator and A.I. programmer.*

**4. With only three main team members working on the game, what sort of difficulties does this bring up? Alternatively, does this make the development process easier in any way?**

> *It’s definitely challenging to make a large game with such a small amount of people, however we also have lots of additional freelance and outsourced help in all aspects of the game from art to code, sound design and many other features that make this possible.*
> *In some ways working with a small team really is beneficial, we can try out and implement new idea’s really quickly. Everyone gets a say in what gets added to the game and it makes for a really rewarding creative working environment.*

**5. You've described the story as being told through gameplay and visuals. Could you give an example of this as it is found in the game?**

> *I’ve always enjoyed games that tell their story visually, and so for this game we’re attempting to tell the story only through visuals and world/gameplay. Imagine yourself dropped in a forest full of cannibals, what happens? That’s the story.*
> *We do have subplots; an old derelict yacht can be found and it’s possible for players to trace the path that these survivors took and ultimately discover what happened to them. We try and fill the game with moments like this, a designed world full of things that have happened, but without a heavy handed voice over or audio logs to tell people explicitly what occurred.*

**6. You've mentioned that your intent with the cannibals was to create enemies that feel emotion and that they weren't inherently evil. How deep does this go? Will cannibals want to get revenge on the player for killing their kind?**

> *We’re trying to make them as realistic as possible, one great example is if you take down an enemy, but don’t finish him off, another cannibal who might have a relationship with that character might throw themselves on the body, blocking you from killing them and in a way offering themselves instead. Its these kind of moments that we hope will elevate the enemy reactions into something much more interesting. We don’t think enemies who are constantly trying to kill the player with no regards to themselves or their friends very interesting. Its definitely a challenge to create this type of A.I., but we hope that approaching A.I. in this way will give players a unique and interesting experience.*

**7. Do the Cannibals that attack you come from a particular location that can be found in-game? Do they only attack at night, or are there reasons for the player to be cautious even in the daylight?**

> *The mostly attack at night, however a big goal of the game is to make the world reactive to everything the player does. If you start going into their caves and killing them whilst they sleep, this may cause them to alter their behaviour in some interesting ways. We found having enemies stick too closely to a routine made them not very scary. Having a sense of unpredictability to them, such as having them stalk you through trees and drop down on the player when least expected can be really scary.*

**8. It's obvious that a lot of work has gone into making the game truly a survival horror with emphasis on survival and horror equally. We know that players can make themselves camouflage, craft weapons, and build shelter. Will players have to search for food and medicine as well? Can they make garments that will help with the weather?**

> *Yep, weather has an effect on everything. Start a small fire and rain will put it out quickly. Cover yourself in mud to help hide from the enemy and rain will wash it off. It’s possible to craft better clothing from the animals and items found in gameworld, as well as craft medicine from plants.*
> *For food we handle it a little differently than most survival games, tying it instead to our strength and stamina systems. We find the idea of not eating for a couple days and then dying really boring from a gameplay point of view, so instead players need to eat well, exercise (chopping down trees for example or engage in combat) and get plenty of rest to keep strength and stamina up. If players don’t eat or sleep eventually their stamina will be so low they will be easy pickings for the forest threats.*

**9. How diverse is the Forest itself? Should players expect a large, wholly lush environment? Are there different areas that are unique from one another?**

> *We want the world to feel like a real place, so we try not to randomly mix biomes together or anything like that. We do have many unique areas however, rocky beaches, swamps, underground lakes and caves. We want more than anything for the world to feel like a coherent place, and not a game world where we change the setting for no reason.*

**10. Do the caves lead to another large area similar to the surface, or is it more of an underground labyrinth type of setup?**

> *There are lots of different caves. Some lead into other caves, others will lead to an opening at the beach. Our tide and ocean system also affects this; some caves will be blocked off by ocean at high tide so players will need to come back at a different time of day to enter.*
> *Before starting this project we went on a real life cave expedition to gather research material and photos. We want the caves to feel as realistic as possible.*

**11. Were there certain concepts that you wanted to include in the game but were unable to do so, whether due to time, hardware or financial constraints? If so, could you tell us about any of those things?**

> *The game isn’t finished yet – Once we release the alpha version we plan to keep working on implementing all of our ideas. As well as involving the community to continue developing the game into something really memorable.*

**12. This game is also being developed for the Oculus Rift. What lead to this choice?**

> *I played some early VR games in the 90’s and was also a big fan of ‘Lawnmower Man’ So hearing about the Rift was really exciting. I think it opens up the path to new types of games, slower paced, less about cinematic storytelling and more about existing in a world, which are exactly the types of games I’m interested in playing and developing.*

**13. What sort of experience does playing on the Rift provide in contrast to a PC monitor?**

> *The feeling of being there, when I first tried the Rift and played the tuscan game demo by Unity, I looked up at the ceiling and imagined how I would feel if a cannibal mutant was up there watching me. The thought was terrifying. For horror games the Rift is going to open up a new type of horror experience unlike anything anyone has experienced in any media before. New horror stories where the enemies are life size and feel real. It’s going to be terrifying.*

**14. Finally, what challenges, if any, did you need to overcome in order to put the game on the Rift?**

> *The biggest issue currently is the low resolution screen and motion sickness. It can be tiring to wear the headset for long periods. We’re hoping to work with Oculus to overcome these issues.*

## Wake & Game

* Published: Jan. 28th 2014
* Wake & Game talking with **Ben Falcone** from Endnight Games
* archived link to the interview

**1. When you were formulating ideas revolving around where exactly you wanted to take this game what made you come up with "The Forest"?**

> *I had previously made a small iPad game that was set in rural town, I found creating all the art and layout really time consuming, and so wanted to have a more natural environment for my next game – something that could be procedurally created, instead of hand placed and so the forest setting seemed perfect.*
> *The forest as a location for a violent scary horror game is also a really exciting idea and early on when i discussed the premise with people everyone was instantly excited about the possibilities.*

**2. The concept you've developed behind the cannibalistic creatures is extremely creative, giving them an actual thought process that can make them react to specific situations in different ways depending on the circumstances. How do you think players are going to respond to this? In my opinion it only spikes my curiosity even more so and I'm already too eager to see what sets off the different behaviors.**

> *We hope players feel some empathy for these creatures. We find the more unpredictable and realistic-seeming we make them and their A.I,. the scarier the experience is.*

**3. The island is both dense and visually dynamic, is it a location that takes awhile to explore when attempting to reach every area available, especially when building in the want to discover the islands lore and secrets?**

> *Creating a world that invites exploration is a key priority for us with the game. Making areas diverse and interesting as well as having a large enough world to make exploration worthwhile is essential for a game like this. Some areas of the game can only be reached by building certain items such as rafts, and we have areas that are accessible by traversing certain caves within the game. Players that explore more, will also be rewarded by finding rare crafting ingredients for creating some of the cooler items in the game.*

**4. Do you feel it was much more of a difficult task working with a smaller development team rather than a large building full of people while you were producing the game? If not is it something that you would rather stick to in the future of Endnight Games?**

> *There are a lot of benefits to working with a small team on a project like this. We can iterate very quickly on ideas, and generally decisions about the game and where it should be heading are quick to nail down. Also personally I think everyone has found it pretty rewarding to take on roles outside their comfort zone, with so few people there is no one to pass the buck so you just have to get things done at the end of the day. I think everyone prefers this style of working, and in future we’d try to keep the team as small as possible.*

**5. You've stated that "I am Legend" and "Cannibal Holocaust" were major influences for you, where there any other sources of inspiration or themes that you considered pulling from when creating the game?**

> *Italian horror in general is a big influence on the game, as well as all the great horror films from the seventies and early eighties. It was always the idea to capture something like "The Hills Have Eyes" or "Zombi" in videogame form. Throw players into a mean violent scary world and let them figure out how to survive.*
> *We also went on a real-life cave trip early on in game development which really helped shape the feel and realism of our cave sections.*

**6. With the game going to be available on the Oculus Rift do you think that is something that is going to take it to the next level horror and survival wise? I can't image how frightening it would be to actually feel like you are 100% immersed in the situation the game puts you in while experiencing it through your own set of eyes, instead of knowing there's that level of safety between you and the screen. The thought of having that barrier removed seems extremely daunting but exciting at the same time.**

> *We’re all very excited about the Oculus Rift, it’s just such a good match for the horror genre. It really does elevate the experience to the next level when all your senses are engaged and your brain is fully immersed in the simulation. When you really feel like you’re in this world and being stalked by creatures at night, and then you find a cave and feel real dread about the possibilities of what's down there. We think the Rift will really make for a uniquely frightening and engaging experience.*
> *At the same time, the 2d version is probably what most people will end up experiencing and even on a flat screen, with headphones on and the lights out the game can be really terrifying.*

**7. What kind of challenges have you faced throughout the development process of the game? Were there any instances when you thought you know what, I don't know how to get us over this hurdle, where do we go from here and how do we move forward with this game?**

> *There are definitely a lot of challenges. Although having a small team has its advantages, it can be a challenge when it comes to just generating the sheer amount of assets and animations needed for a game like this. We’ve had to be creative in how we generate lots of our content, making use of scan data for example, or using photogrammetry techniques to generate rocks and cave surfaces has been invaluable in speeding up the production process.*

**8. The game doesn't involve any voice-overs or obvious instructions that guide you when figuring out what to do, how do you think this is going to impact the players experience as they have to teach themselves what to do, basically the same as they would if they were to be faced with any situation in real life?**

> *It’s true we try to keep hand holding to a minimum in the game. Because of that we try to make all the gameplay systems as simple and straightforward to use as possible. We can also put hints in the world about how things work. For example a locked suitcase might have a rock sitting next to it. Most players can figure out that you can pick up the rock and smash open the case, then they usually see what other things can be smashed in the world.*

**9. Although reaching the ending is not the main focus of this game is there different ways to go about reaching it if that is what you strive for whilst playing? Or, is there one solid path developed that leads the players in that direction without them being fully aware of it?**

How do you keep the players interested in exploration and experiencing the beauty of The Forest while delivering that sense of doom as soon as night falls?**

> *The vision was always to make this really beautiful, interesting world, and then try to use that as a base to scare people. Originally the plan was to only have enemies come out at night, but we’ve found the scariest moments come when you don’t expect the enemies at all. Lots of horror games are set in dark scary locations and it seemed like an interesting idea to try something scary above ground in a pretty location.*

**3. How important is a proper “endgame” to the experience of The Forest? Has it always been a larger goal that the team has kept in mind?**

> *It feels important to me, that there’s this open world, all these things you can do, but then also the option of going straight for the ending. Your son is missing. How do you find him, and what do you do to survive and find out what happened to him? I feel having just a pure survival game with no goal at all would eventually be tiring, and mixing the survival with the very subtle story is really interesting to us. We want players to eventually build up a stash of weapons, cover themselves in armor and the take on the enemy directly, find their missing kid and try and escape the forest.*

**4. The mutants exhibit complex behaviors to say the least. Some will bluff charge you only to back away and stalk you from a distance, yet others will walk directly at you, scream, and presume to smash your skull into a chalky dust. What was the decision behind the complex set of behaviors that the mutants seem to posses, e.g., worshipping at early hours, patrolling, setting up communities, etc.? Does adding a moral scale deter the players from killing every mutant they see?**

> *We wanted to try something different with the AI, and as a team we are always interested in games that try new ideas. From very early on we wanted to try and make these creatures feel alive and dynamic; not knowing exactly what they’re going to do or how they will respond in different situations makes them a lot more interesting and hopefully scary.*

**5. A co­-op mode has been in the works for sometime, according to most sources. Are there any details you could share about the cohabitation with other players and how that will change the dynamic of The Forest. Will your relationship with the other human player(s) necessarily be symbiotic?**

> *We’ve tried to make the world and game as dynamic as possible and feel having another human in there will only further this goal. Work together, or kill each other it’s up to you.*

**Aside from bug fixes and player feedback, what is an advantage of the “Pre­Alpha” public release? Do you worry that some players might be deterred from playing the full future release because of a possibly “buggy” first experience?**

> *It’s definitely a worry for us. We want players to have the best experience possible, and so for most players waiting until final is probably the best idea. Some players, however, really like providing feedback and help to the team, filing bug reports and being part of the development process. It’s also an interesting look behind the curtain for some players who are interested in possible careers in game development at some point, being able to see a game form over time is an interesting experience in itself, but a different one than playing a finished polished release.*

**6. Occulus Rift — The graphical aesthetic of The Forest has a real visceral punch, coaxing players deeper into areas that they might normally avoid. Booming thunder rolls overhead, rain falls in sheets and pools on the ground, a flash of lightning reveals that you are being followed and then you realize you are surrounded. This game seems simultaneously terrifying and mesmerizing with the Occulus Rift. Why did you want to port The Forest to a VR headset?**

> *I’ve always been really excited about VR, 3D and the Rift. I was hoping to have Rift support in for the first version of the alpha release but so many other bugs and gameplay features took priority.We’re hoping to have a Rift version ready in time for the next dev kits and feel that will be a much better experience for players than using the current dev kit, which is limited in a lot of ways. When the VR does work well, and you do get lost in the world it can be a really amazing feeling, looking down at your feet and feeling like you’re actually standing in mud, or seeing a life-size cannibal run jump attack you which can be terrifying.*

**7. Crafting and survival seem to go hand in hand, especially in video games. How deeply do you want the expand the crafting experience to aid the player all the while keeping a balance between skillful playing and white­knuckle panic?**

> *The goal has always been to make something that is really hard, really challenging for players, unless they play smartly, gather resources build weapons,using their wits to survive.We never wanted to make a fast twitch type game and hope as we balance the game, more attentive players will be able to survive for as long as they focus on making good decisions. We want making a series of bad decisions to ultimately lead to the players death.*

**8. Since you have added rafts and houseboats as of V 0.03 are you alluding to the idea of being able to leave the island and explore somewhere behind your humble crash site? Could there be an even greater enemy than sharks lurking in the water? How large would you like to expand the realm of play in The Forest?**

> *Escaping the forest should be the eventual goal of most players, along with finding your missing son, but how exactly you escape isn’t something we want to reveal.*

**9. How do you propose to keep the players interested and coming back once the island has been thoroughly explored and built upon? Are there limits to your character's actions?**

> *We hope the amount of randomness in the world will help with replayability. Co­-op is one place which will really help this, but even as a single player game it’s hopefully a different experience each time players enter the world. Adding more randomness and interesting actions to the creatures, having a bigger world to explore and more things to do will hopefully keep things fresh to players.*

**10. Are there any big changes or additions that you can hint to for the next update?**

> *We’re very close to having a brand new ambient lighting system working in the game, along with a rough pass of global illumination, combined they really bring the visuals together and drastically improve the look and realism of the world. Down the line, opening up new parts of the world and introducing new animals and more importantly the rest of the cannibal types is going to be really exciting and interesting to see.*

**1. At what point in development did you decide that your game was ready to be released as a paid alpha? Why did you feel that was the appropriate time?**

> *When we ran out of money! Some of the fans were yelling at us to release, our private investors were threatening to sue, and we were down to a few hundred dollars in the bank, so we felt it was time.*

**2. What have you learned about the audience for your paid alpha?**

> *The audience seems to be a lot of people really excited about game development in general. We get a lot of enthusiasm, a lot of comments from people who say they wanted to make a game just like ours. We were pretty nervous going into Early Access but found the audience to be actually really great and supportive.*

**3. What have been the benefits of your paid alpha?**

> *The bug reports are really helpful. As a tiny team without a QA department, lots of small things just get missed. Having a large group of people playing each update and emailing us almost instantly with a list of bugs is an amazing resource. The other major benefit is we now have the resources financially to not only complete the game, but to complete it to a standard that would have been impossible relying only on our own savings or private investment.*

**4. What have been the drawbacks and biggest challenges?**

> *The only real drawback has been having to always keep the game somewhat playable. There are times where we want to break everything for a few days to get a new system in, or change the lighting. When the game has to go out to the public 2-3 times a month it means bigger changes are a lot more stressful, and have to be more carefully-timed so as to not break the game drastically for everyone.*

**5. What are key pieces of advice for releasing a paid alpha game?**

> *Don't Starve had a countdown timer for days on the front screen, and let players know exactly when the next update would come. We thought this was an amazing idea and took this a little further and instead added hours, minutes and seconds to how long until next build would be out. I think it's something the public has responded to really well. Every-time you load the game your reminded as a player that the game is still being worked on, and you know when the new update will be out.*

**1.First off, could you briefly elaborate on "The Forest" with just a small sum up of what to expect from the game if having heard nothing about it previously?**

> *You play as the survivor of a passenger jet crash, in an unknown and strange forest. Early on you will scavenge from the plane to survive but as night falls you start to realize you’re not alone.*

**2. I know the trailer shows a crashed plane, but what caused your character to end up in such a scenario?**

> *The answer to this, is one of the many things players can uncover whilst exploring the world. Why are all the other passengers missing, who is hunting him.*

**3. Where did the concept of The Forest come from?**

> *The initial idea was a game set in a forest where you could chop down any tree and build things from the wood. In the original idea the creatures were more mystical and the horror was similar to Blair Witch Project. Over time the concept evolved and introducing an intelligent cannibal threat added a new layer of interest to the survival simulation.*

**4. Furthermore, where did the thought of having ravenous mutants come from?**

> *I’ve always been a fan of Italian horror, in the 80’s there were a bunch of really bad cannibal horror films made. Although I was never really a fan of those films, the imagery from them stuck with me, and for this game seemed a perfect fit.*

> *Players can fight the mutants directly using crude weapons made from sticks and stones, or spend time building elaborate traps. > *Changing the environment has been one of our main goals in this project, Players can cut down every plant and tree in the game world, completely changing the look of the world. **7. What will be the balance between crafting items to survive and just exploring the luscious jungle?**

Little unexpected things become terrifying, lifting a rock and a small spider scurrying out from underneath causes me to jump back slightly every time it happens.*

## GamingBolt 2

* Published: Nov. 5th 2018
* GamingBolt talking with **Anna Terekhova** from Endnight Games

**1. One of The Forest's biggest strengths has to be its atmosphere, how it makes you feel so isolated all the time. The setting obviously plays a huge part in that, but how do you strike that balance of achieving that sort of atmosphere without every making it too overbearing?**

> *The game is really separated into two parts. We have the cave systems under the world that are dark and scary, and then the above ground which tends to have a much brighter, light mood. These two elements work together and we hope stop the horror element from ever feeling like its too much for players.*
> *In multiplayer the game tends to be much less scary, that is until you get separated from your friends.*

**2. Are there any stories or films or games that influenced you directly while you were working on The Forest?**

> *Originally the old Italian cannibal films from the 80’s inspired the setting, however over time the story and game has developed to become its own thing.*

**3. “Empty open world” is a criticism that we hear levelled at a great many games, but in the case of The Forest, that seems to work in its favour. What was the process of deciding what kinds of activities to give to players while keeping the world true to that sense of isolation?**

> *We didn’t want to fill the world with fetch quests, or find item type missions that fill many open world games. Instead, players decide themselves what they want to do and then the things in the world occur, often changing the players’ plans. Because every plant and tree can be chopped down, and then items collected, you’re constantly doing something as you move through the world, chopping, collecting, chasing rabbits, finding a cave entrance to explore etc.*

**4. Can we expect any notable changes or additions in the game's PS4 version?**

> *There are a ton of improvements we’ve done for the PS4 release. We’re also planning on bringing these improvements over to the PC in a patch update. Some of the improvements include new GPU grass for better visuals and performance. Most of the trees and plant are have been tweaked and improved visually. We have new billboard shaders for a better looking distant trees, and a bunch of bug fixes and small improvements to the UI and gameplay.*

**5. What would you say are the biggest things the multiplayer co-op adds to the experience? Do you think the multiplayer can work against the game's atmosphere, or that feeling of having to survive all alone in this harsh, deadly environment?**

> *Multiplayer adds a level of extra randomness to everything which we feel really suits the game. You now have an extra person in the world who is maybe constructing things and wants to help you fight off the enemies, or maybe wants to destroy your base when you’re back is turned.*
> *We do watch a lot of playthroughs of players on Youtube or Twitch, and people seem to really enjoy experiencing the game together, building structures, exploring caves and fighting off enemies.*

**6. The Forest was in Early Access for four years. How much would you say that helped shape the final product into what it is today? Are there any major features or mechanics in the game that you can point to and identify as a result of the feedback you got from the game's Early Access years?**

> *We took the early access experience extremely seriously, and listened to all the feedback from players. We’re really grateful to everyone who played the game in early access and especially those who took the time to offer comments, both positive and negative. The Forest became a much better game with the guidance and feedback of the players who were actually playing the game over that 4 year period.*
> *It’s difficult to point to a single feature, every element of the game was tweaked based on player response. If elements of the story were not making much sense we would add more clues, if a part of the gameplay was frustrating we would try and tweak to improve it.*

**7. The AI of the cannibals plays a huge part in helping players what they're about, and it's fair to say that it does a lot of stuff through gameplay that many other games would choose to do through storytelling or exposition. How hard was it to get that part right?**

> *We’re not interested at all in story told through NPCs or cutscenes really. Our game has almost no dialogue and tries to tell the story visually instead. We have a few lines of dialogue, but none of it is important to understand the game.*
> *Instead of a traditional story, we wanted to just have a situation players are in, in this case, you crash land in a mysterious forest, and there are unfriendly locals. The real story is unique to each player and how they chose to play the game and experience the world.*

**8. Do you have any plans for a sequel?**

> *We’d want to really change things up for a sequel. When The Forest came out it was really a unique game, since then there have been a lot of similar survival games released. We do have ideas of what a completely new take on a survival game could be (unlike anything else that exists currently) and are really excited to start exploring those ideas.*

**9. Any plans for adding stuff like PvP, or maybe a sandbox mode where players can just build their bases to their hearts’ content?**

> *Once players finish the game they can unlock the creative mode. Additionally players who don’t wish to deal with enemies can play in peaceful mode.*
> *We discussed PVP frequently, however we feel The Forest works better as a friendly co-op game. We like that the multiplayer component tends to be a more friendly experience, where you help each other build, survive and live in a world.*

**10. Does the game will feature PS4 Pro-specific enhancements? What can players expect if they are playing the game on PS4 Pro? Is 4K/60fps on the cards?**

> *Both the original and Pro versions will run at 30fps. With extra visual improvements on the Pro compared to the original PS4.*

**11. From a development perspective, how do you find the PS4 Pro to be? With so much GPU power, we are sure you must be doing some amazing things.**

> *There’s always challenges in bringing a game from one platform to another, in this case from PC.However the pro has made this process a lot easier than it otherwise would have been. The bulk of our optimization work has been geared towards making sure the original PS4 offers the same experience as the higher powered console, or a high spec PC.*

**12. How is the game running on the original PS4, frame rate and resolution wise?**

> *We’re targeting 30fps and currently locking down final resolution. The tricky part with The Forest is players can build their own structures, and so one of the major challenges has also been optimizing and loading buildings so that when players start building massive towers and bridges across the map things still run well.*

**13. Do you have any plans to bring it on the Xbox One?**

> *We’d love to bring the game to Xbox in the future.*

**14. Why did you go console exclusive with PS4?**

> *Sony had first contacted us back when the initial trailer for the game was released. We were able to build up a really good relationship with them, and knowing that as a small team we would only be able to focus on one console at a time being exclusive for a short period of time made sense.*

**15. Next-gen is coming sooner or later. From a development perspective, what is your biggest expectation from PS5 and Xbox Scarlett?**

> *We’re really excited about upcoming consoles, but to be honest we don’t know much about them at this point.*

### How to Survive The Forest, Out Tomorrow on PS4

* Published: Nov. 5th 2018
* by Anna Terekhova from Endnight Games
* original link to the blog

The Forest isn't like other games. You won't be given tutorials or guidance, there are no NPCs or missions. You're dropped into a world and it's up to your creativity and abilities to survive. You'll probably die a lot, especially at first.  
Survive in this new type of game unlike anything else with these hints and tips:

**CRAFT**

> *One of the simplest weapons you can make is a CLUB. This weapon is effective in combat but won’t chop trees. To make a club, simply find an enemy cannibal and kill him. Once he’s dead, chop his head off and then build a fire. Place the head on the fire and wait for it to burn away the skin and flesh. Now, take your freshly cooked skull, find a stick and shove the skull onto the stick. You now have an effective weapon against the bad guys.*

**BUILD AN EFFIGY**

> *Another effective survival tactic is to fully dismember all of the enemies you kill. With your new club this should be much easier. Take as many body parts as you can carry and decorate stick effigies with them. You can then light these all on fire as a warning to others not to mess with you. Extra hint: Build a log sled to transport lots of bodies at once to make harvesting them easier.*

**FOOD**

> *It’s important to keep your energy up, and that means eating. You can hunt deer and rabbits, or maybe find a boar or racoon. Berries are available but some might be poisonous. Another option is to cook and eat the body parts of the enemies you’ve killed. Body parts can be cooked by drying on a drying rack, placed directly on a fire, or for an energy-boosting stew add a leg to a pot of boiling water with some mushrooms and berries.*

**BUILDINGS**

> *A good base needs a good fence. You have a few options here, you can make a weak fence out of sticks, a strong wall out of stones, or take a dead enemy, drop him on a fire, wait for the body to burn away, and then collect his or her bones. These bones can make a great decorative fence that’s a lot stronger than regular wood.*

**UPGRADES**

> *Finding your weapons too weak against the ever encroaching enemy threat that cannot be reasoned with? Weapons can all be upgraded. First, chop at a tree to receive some TREE SAP. Now, bash away at some enemies — TEETH should fly out. You can now use the tree sap with the teeth to make your weapons even deadlier.*

**ARMOR**

> *Smash open the skull of a lizard and then cut off it’s skin to wrap around your body for an effective light armor. Combine skin with leaves for a stealth armor. Want even more powerful armor? Slice away at the corpses of some of the game’s bigger monsters and wear their skin as protection.*

**STAY CLEAN**

> *Covered in blood from fighting and chopping enemies? Make sure to find a clear water source to wash off. Getting blood inside your cuts won’t be good for you.*

**STILL FINDING THE GAME TOO HARD?**

> *Invite a friend to play with you. Sure, the enemies will scale up in difficulty as a result, but going online is a great way to learn the ins and outs of survival as well as making new friends. Chop, build, and fight to survive in The Forest, out tomorrow at PlayStation Store for $19.99.*
